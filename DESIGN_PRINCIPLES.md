# Pearwaa Design Principles

## Product thesis

Pearwaa is a calm space where flowers become a language for emotions that
are difficult to express with words. Whether you're discovering what you
feel, creating a bouquet, or sending a heartfelt letter, every interaction
exists to help you say something meaningful — gently, intentionally, and
without pressure.

Pearwaa is not a flower encyclopedia. It is not a bouquet builder. It is
not a letter app. Every design decision must support the thesis above —
if a UI element, animation, layout, or interaction doesn't help someone
express, understand, or preserve an emotion, reconsider it. The goal is
not "beautiful UI." The goal is an experience that feels gentle, personal,
warm, and intentional. Avoid trying to impress the user — make them feel
comfortable.

## Principles

- Flowers are the language, not the product.
- Calm over engagement.
- Reflection over productivity.
- Expression over optimization.
- Meaning before information.
- Beauty should never create pressure.
- Every interaction should feel handcrafted.
- Reduce visual noise.
- Prioritize emotional clarity over feature visibility.
- If a feature doesn't help someone express, understand, or preserve an
  emotion, it probably doesn't belong.

## How this shows up in practice

- **Purple is an accent, not the identity.** Reserve it for primary
  actions, selected states, important highlights, seals, and emotional
  moments (the letter-sealing animation, most of all). It should feel
  meaningful because it's rare. Everywhere else: warm ivory backgrounds,
  soft charcoal (not pure black) type, muted greens, and the natural
  flower colors already established in the watercolor art direction.
- **Copy avoids generic software language.** No "Submit," "Success,"
  "Done," "Saved," "Uploaded." Prefer emotionally specific phrasing —
  "Your flowers are on their way," "Open when you're ready," "Take your
  time," "Written with care."
- **No dark patterns around optional moments.** Skipping something
  optional (a reflection, a step) should carry the same visual weight as
  continuing — never a discouraged, greyed-out, or guilt-tripping "skip."
- **Motion resembles nature, not UI conventions.** Flowers breathe, paper
  settles, petals drift, letters unfold. Ease-out movement, soft fades,
  gentle transforms, layered timing. Avoid bounce, flashy transitions,
  exaggerated scale, excessive blur. If an animation doesn't reinforce the
  calm tone, cut it — don't just tone it down.
- **Never gamified.** No streaks, scores, progress bars, or achievement
  fanfare applied to anything emotional (reflections, letters, meanings).
  The Encyclopedia's quiet collection/achievement notes are the outer edge
  of what's acceptable, and even those stay understated.
